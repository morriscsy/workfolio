1. Executive summary

Under the stated multiplicative rules, the model shows a strong positive expectancy (average gain per trade before compounding). In 100 Monte Carlo paths, nearly all finished above the $1,400 start and most at least doubled. Outcomes are highly right-skewed: a typical (median) path ends near $335.4k (+23,857% / ~239.6× start), while the mean (~$1.30M, +92,757% / ~928.6×) is lifted by a small number of very strong paths.

Risk caveat: median peak-to-trough drawdown is still ~50% (95th percentile ~73%). The edge compounds, but the equity curve is not smooth — capital policy and psychology matter as much as the math. This model is not a live trading plan.

6. Multiplier analysis (×1.30 win / ×0.96 loss)

TRADE SIM’s baseline uses asymmetric multipliers: a win scales capital by ×1.30 (+30%); a loss scales by ×0.96 (−4%). That asymmetry — not win rate alone — is why the Monte Carlo right tail is so large.

Win multiplier ×1.30 +30% of current capital per win trade
Loss multiplier ×0.96 −4% of current capital per loss trade

Why the asymmetry matters

  • One win more than recovers one loss: after a loss then a win, capital is ×0.96 × 1.30 = ×1.248 (+24.8% net). Losses do not “cancel” wins one-for-one.
  • Geometric mean per trade at 22% WR: 1.300.22 × 0.960.78 ≈ ×1.0262 (+2.62%/trade). Over 200 trades that compounds to roughly the 10k-path median (~$248k / +17,593%).
  • Path dependence of drawdowns, not of final capital: for a fixed win count, ending capital is the same product of multipliers regardless of order. Sequence only changes the path’s peak-to-trough drawdown — which is why median max DD still sits near ~52%.
  • Breakeven win rate is low (~13.5%) because each win pays +30% while each loss costs only 4%. At 22% WR the model has ~8.5 percentage points of cushion vs geometric breakeven.

7. Key takeaways

  1. Expectancy is strong under stated rules — both arithmetic (+3.48%) and geometric (+2.62%) edge sit well above breakeven; median return is about +23,857%.
  2. Risk-adjusted ratios are model-implied (rf=0) — per-trade Sharpe ~0.25, Sortino ~0.99, IR vs flat = Sharpe; period Calmar ~459× (median return / median DD). See §5 — not live or index alpha.
  3. Plan to the median, not the mean — a fat right tail inflates the average; the 100-path median ($335.4k / +23,857%) is the better planning number than the mean ($1.30M / +92,757%).
  4. ~50% drawdowns are normal in this model — risk budget and sizing should assume multi-period pain even with positive expectancy (median max DD 52%, P95 ~73%).
  5. ×1.30 / ×0.96 asymmetry drives compounding — one win more than recovers one loss (×1.248 net); ending capital depends on win count, while sequence mainly shapes drawdowns.
  6. This is a capital-growth simulation, not a full trading plan — it assumes fixed % multipliers and independent outcomes. Operational use needs entry/exit rules, costs, and regime filters.
  7. Sensitivity risk — if realized win rate drifts toward ~15%, or losses exceed ~4% after fees/slippage, the right tail (and those large return %) shrinks quickly.

Recommended next steps

  • Validate that live trades realize ~×1.30 / ×0.96 after fees and slippage.
  • Convert the detailed entry/exit plan into rule-based backtests.
  • Stress-test a grid: win rate 15–25% × loss multiplier 0.94–0.98.
  • Define a capital policy that tolerates ~50–65% peak drawdowns before scaling.

8. Method notes

Each path starts at $1,400. For 200 independent trades, equity is multiplied by 1.30 with probability 22%, otherwise by 0.96. Return % throughout this brief is (final capital ÷ 1,400 − 1) × 100. The brief focuses on 100 re-simulations; a 10,000-path check was used only to confirm distribution shape. Indicator sample seed = 42 (numpy Generator); per-trade Sharpe/Sortino/IR use closed-form moments with rf = 0. Dollar distribution KPIs in §§1–3 are unchanged from the original model run.
