Research summary

Which rule to trust

Market buy, 30% target (Market TP30). Market buy, average exit (Market MA150). Limit order, 23% floor (Limit MA23). Midpoint limit, 30% target (Mid TP30). Midpoint, 15-minute confirm (Mid 15m). Midpoint, 34% target (Mid TP34). Added buys, shared stop (Mid Pyramid).

On a 5-minute chart, Midpoint, 34% target is the result I would trust.

5-minute 34% take profit 50 of 77 above zero

Morris, these seven rules all buy a dip and stay long. They use the 150-bar average and the 200-bar average, on 77 names, on a 1-minute chart and a 5-minute chart. A 2% stop closes the trade if price falls 2% from the fill. The median is the middle name, so one huge winner does not decide the result. A 2X fund aims to move about twice as much as the stock or index it tracks. Every figure below was counted again from the workbook.

04How the seven differ

Market buy, 30% target is the start. Each of the other six changes one or two things. Read down a branch. What I do not mention is unchanged.

Market buy, 30% target

Base

Buy at the market once price is back above the 150-bar and 200-bar averages. The stop is 2% under the fill. The target is +30% on the same bar size as the chart. One entry. No early exit.

Branch A · still a market buy

From the base

Market buy, average exit. Same market buy, same 30% target. Two changes. It can also buy when the averages were sloping down. And it sells on a touch of the 150-bar average as soon as the trade is green. That early exit is why this one lags.

Limit order, 23% floor goes one step further. Same signals as Market buy, average exit, including the buy when the averages were sloping down. The order is a limit between the 150-bar and 200-bar averages. The exit on the 150-bar average waits until the trade is up at least 23%.

Branch B · limit at the midpoint

From the base

Midpoint limit, 30% target. No buy when the averages were sloping down, and no exit on the 150-bar average. The only change from the start is the order: a limit between the two averages, cancelled if it is still open after 5 bars. The 2% stop and the 30% target stay on the chart’s own bar.

Midpoint, 15-minute confirm. Same as Midpoint limit, 30% target. The 30% target fills only after a closed 15-minute bar has traded through it. The 15-minute bar does not close the trade by itself.

Midpoint, 34% target. Same as Midpoint, 15-minute confirm. The on-chart panel for the stop and the target does not change the orders. The saved settings still start at 30%. This test used 34%.

Added buys, shared stop. Same as Midpoint, 15-minute confirm, but a new signal can add while a trade is open, up to 3 entries. Every add uses the first entry’s 2% stop, not a fresh stop under its own fill. Each entry still targets 30%, and that target waits for a closed 15-minute bar. This one was not rerun at 34%.

SPCX and SKHY started trading in the middle of this window, so I leave them out of this description.

One-day momentum is not what separates winners from losers. The link between today’s return and yesterday’s is near zero on almost every name, winners included. OKLO and OKLL are the exception, at -0.23, so yesterday’s move tended to reverse.

The daily path that fits a long-only buy-the-dip rule with a 30% target is a low close early, a later close far above that low, and an ending close that keeps a good part of the gain. INTW, SNDK, INTC, and SMCI did that. The window low was 30 Mar on all four. From that low the later peak was INTW +824.66% (5.6675 to 52.405), SNDK +307.86%, INTC +242.17%, and SMCI +138.22%. MSTR is only the hole and the recovery. The close went from 82.31 on 26 Jun to 168.50 on 21 Sep, and it finished +15.78% versus 23 Mar. It had more down days than up days, 59 against 75.
